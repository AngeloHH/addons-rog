# Addons ROG

A long time ago, I joined a development group working on a Cuban World of Warcraft
server. It was full of people who knew an incredible amount about what they did.
Every Friday, they would meet to discuss their progress and agree on the tasks for
the following week. That was my first experience being part of a team, with people
working together toward something they cared about.

Unfortunately, I gave priority to other things. Little by little, I left myself
with no time for the group, and I eventually walked away from the project. Looking
back, I wish I had made more room for that experience and the people who were part
of it.

Some time later, a friend asked whether I would like to help them again. This time,
they needed a website where players could upload and share addons. I quickly got
in touch with one of the administrators, who gave me a template and the freedom
to build what they were looking for. I was excited to have another opportunity to
contribute, and I threw myself into the work. It took me about a week to complete
that first version. It passed an initial visual review by one of the moderators,
and only a few details remained before they could put it to use.

Then the network I used to connect went through structural changes, and I lost my
connection to that network. I became completely disconnected from that world. Months passed, and
by then I had also graduated from school. The website became another unfinished
project, left behind while life moved on.

That has stayed with me. Whenever I look back, I am bothered by how many projects
I have set aside for one reason or another. Some were interrupted by circumstances;
others were the result of my own decisions. Over time, those unfinished projects
started to make me doubt my ability to finish the work I enjoy so much. I know how
excited I can be at the beginning, and I know how much care I can put into building
something. Still, it is difficult to feel confident when so many of those efforts
remain unfinished.

Recently, I found the source code for the old template. So I made some time and came back to it. Returning after so long has given me the
chance to revisit the work with more patience and a clearer idea of what I want
to build. There is something comforting about being able to pick up a project that
once felt lost and keep going from where it stopped.

Thank you, El Gato, for welcoming me into the WoW development group, even though
I was pretty immature back then. Thank you, KZKG^Gaara, for giving me a second
chance. And Casey, about your bet with Link: you won. I honestly thought I'd stick
around in that world with you all.
